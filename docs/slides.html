Two Counties, Two Crisis Points

Western Kansas faces two distinct agricultural failure modes: Summer Bare-Fallow Wind Stripping vs. Groundwater Mining.

Northwest Dryland Hub FIPS: 20193
Thomas County (Colby, KS)
4 Months
Continuous Bare Ground Exposure (NDVI < 0.20)
  • Baseline System: Winter Wheat–Fallow (1 crop every 2 years).
  • The Failure Mode: After July harvest, fields sit completely bare through intense 35°C+ summer heat. Over 80% of summer rainfall evaporates.
  • Wind Detachment Hazard: Dry, pulverized Keith silt loam topsoil is exposed to 30–50 mph spring winds, triggering severe Dust Bowl-style wind erosion.
Southwest Irrigated Hub FIPS: 20055
Finney County (Garden City, KS)
97.3 ft
Ogallala Water Table Drop Since 1990
  • Baseline System: Continuous Irrigated Corn under center-pivot systems.
  • The Failure Mode: Massive groundwater extraction (18–20 acre-inches/yr) exceeds natural recharge by orders of magnitude.
  • Regulatory & Financial Ruin: GRACE-FO confirms negative terrestrial water storage; producers face state LEMA pumping caps and rising pumping electricity bills.

Harnessing Multi-Sensor NASA Data

CampoWechsel synthesizes 5 active NASA satellite missions into continuous field-level indicators.

🛰️ MODIS (MOD13Q1 / 250m)
NDVI Vegetative Armor

16-day composite time series mapping canopy green-up and bare-field duration ($\text{NDVI} < 0.20$). Proves canopy cooling maintains topsoil 19.4°F cooler than bare ground.

☀️ NASA POWER Daily
Heat & Solar Agroclimatology

10-year daily weather records tracking extreme heat stress ($T_{\max} \ge 35^\circ\text{C}$: 33.9 d/yr Colby, 42.1 d/yr Garden City), growing degree days, and dry spells.

💧 SMAP & MERRA-2
Root-Zone Soil Wetness

Standardized root-zone moisture anomalies ($Z$-scores) detecting flash droughts (2018, 2022) to evaluate which rotations survive moisture deficits.

🌊
NASA GRACE & GRACE-FO Gravimetry
Tracks regional terrestrial water storage loss across the High Plains aquifer boundary, validating the urgency of groundwater curtailment.
📐
NASA SRTM 30m Digital Elevation
Quantifies tableland slope (<1%), mathematically proving that erosion is caused by wind particle detachment, not water runoff.

Decision Science & Architecture Deterministic & Transparent MCDA

The Scientific Decision Engine

No opaque black-box AI: Every rotation recommendation is computed via transparent, peer-reviewed agronomic formulas.

Empirical Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) Formula
$$S_j = \sum_{i=1}^{8} w_i \cdot r_{ij} \cdot a_{ij}$$
Composite Score = Normalized Farmer Priority Weights ($w_i$) × K-State Baseline Suitability ($r_{ij}$) × Dynamic NASA Anomaly Multipliers ($a_{ij}$)
1. Priority Weights ($w_i$)

Farmers customize 3 simple sliders (0–10) which map directly to 8 core criteria (weights strictly sum to 1.0):

  • Save Water: Water efficiency (65%), Drought buffer (35%).
  • Protect Soil: Cover duration (35%), Wind erosion armor (35%), Organic matter (30%).
  • Earn Income: Net margins (50%), Pest break (25%), Nitrogen credit (25%).
2. K-State Baseline ($r_{ij}$)

1 to 5 agronomic ratings calibrated directly from published field trials by Kansas State University Research & Extension:

  • MF-2573: Dryland Cropping Systems
  • SRL-138: Kansas Soil Cover Surveys
  • MF-2895: Limited-Irrigation Systems
  • MF-3244: High Plains Cover Crops
3. Dynamic Modifiers ($a_{ij}$)

Continuous factors ($0.70$ to $1.10$) computed live from NASA satellite observations:

  • Bare Months $\ge 4$: Fallow penalized ($a=0.70$), living cover rewarded ($a=1.10$).
  • Heat Days $\ge 30$: Corn penalized ($a=0.75$), C4 sorghum rewarded ($a=1.10$).
  • Moisture Deficit: Drought-tolerant crops rewarded ($a=1.08$).
